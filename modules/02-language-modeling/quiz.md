# Module 02 — Knowledge check

Closed book, about 25 minutes. Solutions: [`solutions/02-language-modeling.md`](../../solutions/02-language-modeling.md).

1. **(Concept)** Write the chain-rule factorisation of $P(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4)$. Is it an approximation?

2. **(Calculation)** A model has a validation loss of 1.386 nats per token. Give its perplexity and its loss in bits per token. If each token is on average 4 bytes, what is the loss in bits per byte?

3. **(Calculation)** A language over 4 symbols is generated by independent draws with probabilities (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/8). What is the lowest cross-entropy (in bits per symbol) any model can reach? What does a model that predicts uniformly score?

4. **(Concept)** What is teacher forcing? Give one advantage and one consequence at generation time.

5. **(Code reading)** In nanochat's data loader, `row_buffer` has shape `(B, T + 1)`. Why `T + 1`, and what are `row_buffer[:, :-1]` and `row_buffer[:, 1:]`?

6. **(Concept)** During generation, why is only the last position's output used, when the model computes outputs for all positions?

7. **(Experimental reasoning)** A 10-character n-gram model scores 100 % on simple agreement items and 0 % on items with an attractor. A colleague concludes "it has not learned agreement at all". Give a more precise description of what it has learned and why.

8. **(Concept)** State the two independent reasons why n-gram models cannot be rescued by making $n$ large.

9. **(Calculation)** A character-level model of Pocket reports a validation loss of 0.290 nats/char on a large validation set. What do you conclude?

10. **(Synthesis)** "A language model has no memory beyond its context window." Explain how a chat application nevertheless holds a multi-turn conversation.

## Mastery criteria

- [ ] Compute the probability of a short sentence under a given grammar or n-gram table, both whole and token by token.
- [ ] Convert between nats, bits and perplexity without looking up formulas.
- [ ] Draw the input/target alignment for a sequence and explain what each position is trained to do.
- [ ] Explain the generation loop and name three differences between training and generation.
- [ ] Explain, with the attractor experiment, why fixed-context counting fails.
- [ ] At least 8 of 10 quiz questions correct.

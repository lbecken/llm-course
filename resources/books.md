# Books and long-form texts

| Book | Access | Level | Use with | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jurafsky and Martin, [*Speech and Language Processing*](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/), 3rd edition draft | free online | introductory–intermediate | Modules 02, 03, 05, 10 | The standard NLP text. Its chapters on n-gram models, neural language models, Transformers and evaluation are careful where blog posts are quick. Updated regularly: cite the chapter title, not the number. |
| Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville, [*Deep Learning*](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/) (2016) | free online | intermediate · historical | Modules 01, 07 | Rigorous treatment of feed-forward networks, optimisation and regularisation. Predates Transformers; the mathematics of chapters 6–8 is unchanged. |
| Prince, [*Understanding Deep Learning*](https://udlbook.github.io/udlbook/) (2023) | free online | intermediate | Parts I–II | A modern textbook with excellent figures; chapters on Transformers, training and initialisation complement Modules 05–07. |
| Raschka, [*Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)*](https://www.manning.com/books/build-a-large-language-model-from-scratch) (2024) | commercial | introductory | after Parts I, II, IV | Builds a GPT-2-style model, pretraining and fine-tuning step by step in code. A second voice on the same construction, with a classic (not nanochat-style) architecture: comparing the two is instructive. |
| Sutton and Barto, [*Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*](http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html), 2nd edition (2018) | free online | intermediate | Module 15 | Chapter 13 for policy gradients; chapters 1–3 for the vocabulary of agents, rewards and returns. |
| Russell and Norvig, [*Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*](https://aima.cs.berkeley.edu/), 4th edition (2020) | commercial | introductory | Modules 18, 19 | Agents, environments and search as defined long before language models. Part V of this course is that framework with a model as one component. |
| MacKay, [*Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms*](https://www.inference.org.uk/mackay/itila/book.html) (2003) | free online | intermediate · historical | Module 02 | Entropy, coding and the link between compression and prediction, explained with unusual clarity. Chapters 1–6. |
| Huyen, [*AI Engineering*](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/ai-engineering/9781098166298/) (2025) | commercial | introductory | Parts V–VI | Building applications on foundation models: evaluation, retrieval, agents, serving. Broad where this course is deep; useful for the production concerns Module 21 only names. |
| Beyer et al. (eds.), [*Site Reliability Engineering*](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/) (2016) | free online | intermediate | Module 21 | Monitoring, troubleshooting and capacity for running systems. Not about ML; entirely relevant to operating one. |

How to use books with this course: the modules are self-contained, so read a chapter
*after* the corresponding module, as a second explanation and a source of exercises.
Where a book and the course disagree on a detail of a modern model, check the pinned
nanochat source: architectures have moved since most books were written.
